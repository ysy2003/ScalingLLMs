Play/life balance *

Greg Bayer :

Working for a startup usually means putting in more hours
than others. Recently, I spent two days on less than 3
hours of sleep in order to push out our new Pulse.me
release.

Chasing after dreams is an essential part of my life. The
feeling of fulfillment I get from doing so makes me a much
happier / more content person, and this in turn positively
affects my relationships.

I've argued before that hours are not a measure of
productivity, but that's not Greg's claim in this post. He's
saying, quite rightly, that working on your startup is not work,
it's play - and so, unlike working stupid hours on a job,
working stupid hours on a startup is a blessing, not a curse.

I'm reminded of an old saying:

Why work for someone else from nine to five for a daily
wage, when you can work twenty-four hours a day for
yourself for free?

So which view is right? is
